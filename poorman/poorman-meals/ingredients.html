Know what you're buying

Ingredient Spotlights

The cheap staples worth always having on hand, and how to get the most value out of each one.

Pantry staple

Mung Beans (Monggo)

~₱90 / kg Keeps 6+ months dry High in protein & fiber

One of the best peso-for-nutrition buys at any market. A single kilo, bought dry, can stretch across four or five meals when simmered into soup, stew, or even a simple sweet dessert (ginataang monggo). It needs no refrigeration and barely spoils, so it's worth buying in bulk when you have a little extra to spend.

Leafy green

Kangkong (Water Spinach)

~₱15–25 / bunch Cooks in under 5 min High in iron & vitamin A

Grows fast and sells cheap almost everywhere in the country, which makes it one of the most reliable vegetables to build a meal around. It wilts quickly once cooked, so add it last and cook it fast — over-boiling turns it slimy and washes out both flavor and nutrients.

Everyday protein

Eggs

~₱8–9 each Keeps 3–4 weeks Complete protein

Still the cheapest complete protein you can buy at a wet market, and one of the most versatile. Scrambled into fried rice, hard-boiled into a stew, or fried plain over rice — a dozen eggs can genuinely cover a week of protein if you rotate how you use them.

Smoked fish

Tinapa

~₱15–20 / piece Keeps 5–7 days chilled Big flavor per peso

Smoked fish carries a dish the way a small amount of bacon does in other cuisines — it's not the main event, it's the flavor engine. One piece, flaked, can season an entire pan of fried rice or a pot of vegetables, which makes its per-meal cost much lower than the sticker price suggests.
