Ginisang Monggo

Mung beans simmered down with garlic, onion, tomato, and whatever greens are cheapest this week. It's the dish that taught half this blog how to stretch a grocery budget.

Cost per serving ₱18
Total cost ₱72
Serves 4
Time 35 min

Ingredients

  • Mung beans (monggo)1 cup
  • Garlic, minced4 cloves
  • Onion, chopped1 small
  • Tomato, chopped1 medium
  • Cooking oil2 tbsp
  • Water4 cups
  • Kangkong or malunggay leaves1 bunch
  • Fish sauce (patis)2 tbsp
  • Ground black pepperto taste
  • Chicharon or fried tinapa flakesoptional, to top
Budget swap No malunggay or kangkong on hand? Any cheap leafy green works — even the tops of radish or sweet potato leaves, which are often given away free at the market.

Method

  1. Rinse the mung beans and pick out any small stones or debris. Boil in the water over medium heat for about 20 minutes, until the beans start to soften and split.
  2. While the beans cook, heat the oil in a separate pan. Sauté the garlic until golden, then add the onion and cook until soft.
  3. Add the tomato and cook down until it breaks apart and turns saucy, about 3 minutes.
  4. Pour the sautéed garlic, onion, and tomato into the pot of beans. Stir together and simmer another 10 minutes, until the beans are fully soft and the mixture has thickened slightly.
  5. Season with fish sauce and pepper. Stir in the greens and cook just until wilted, about 2 minutes — don't overcook or they'll turn bitter.
  6. Ladle into bowls and top with crushed chicharon or fried tinapa flakes if you have them. Serve hot with rice.
Make it stretch This keeps well for 3 days in the fridge and actually tastes better the next day. Double the batch on a rest day and you've got lunch sorted for half the week.
